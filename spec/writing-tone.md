# Writing tone

How copy on this site should sound, for anyone (human or AI) writing or
editing text here.

## The tone

Humble, friendly, individual. This is one person's site, not a brand's —
it should read like a person talking to another person, not like ad copy.

- Use **"I"**, **"you"**, and **"we"**. Write in first person about
  yourself and your work; address the reader directly; use "we" when
  describing something you and the reader (or you and a client/team) do
  together. Avoid the distancing third person ("Joel Parker Henderson
  is a software engineer who…") outside of `<title>`/meta descriptions
  and JSON-LD, where third person is structurally required.
- Be humble about claims. State what you've done and let it speak for
  itself, rather than asserting how good, holds-up, or impressive it
  is. "I build things that get used" beats "I build software that
  holds up under pressure."
- Be friendly. Warmth over polish. It's fine to sound like a person
  who is glad you're reading this, not a company reciting positioning.

## No sentence fragments

Every sentence needs a subject and a verb. Don't write clipped,
ad-copy-style noun phrases strung together for punchy rhythm — they
read as arrogant, curt, and abrupt, not confident.

**Anti-pattern** (what not to do):

> Engineering leadership that holds up under pressure. Decisions that
> don't unravel. Teams that stay healthy. Software built to be reused,
> not shipped once and forgotten.

None of those four sentences has a subject doing something — they're
fragments dressed up as a list of promises, and they sound like a
company tagline, not a person.

**Better**: write complete sentences, with "I"/"you"/"we" as the
subject where it fits naturally:

> I lead engineering in a way that holds up under pressure — I want
> the decisions I make to still make sense a year later, and the teams
> I lead to stay healthy while we get there. When I publish software,
> I build it to be reused, not shipped once and forgotten.

This doesn't mean every sentence must be long. Short sentences are
good — see the site's existing "one idea per sentence" convention for
the homepage intro. The rule is complete sentences, not necessarily
long ones: "I like short sentences." is complete; "Short sentences."
is a fragment.

## Applying this

When writing or reviewing prose — hero text, page introductions,
philosophy/bio paragraphs, anything presented as narrative — check it
against both rules above before publishing. If existing copy violates
either one, it's worth fixing even if it wasn't the thing you were
asked to change.

**Exception:** short UI captions — tile blurbs, button/CTA labels, nav
labels — are a different genre and stay terse by convention (e.g. "Read
the Guide →", or a one-line tile caption under a card title). They're
not "someone talking about themselves," so the no-fragments rule
doesn't apply to them the same way. Use judgment: if it reads like a
person narrating, it needs to be a real sentence; if it reads like a
UI label, brevity is fine.
